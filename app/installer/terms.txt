3. YOUR DATA, ONLINE AND OFFLINE MODE

MyMTGO records your matches on your computer whether or not you have a
MyMTGO account. You can turn offline mode on at any time in Settings, Data
& Privacy. Leaving offline mode switched off means you agree to your data
being sent as described below.

Kept on this computer

  Everything the app records is stored in a database on this computer: your
  matches, games, decks, leagues, drafts, notes, archetypes and settings. It
  is built by reading files MTGO writes here: mtgo.log, your deck files,
  MTGO's game log files, its match history files when you import them, and
  its card files for token names. A MyMTGO 0.x database is only read, never
  changed, when you import it.

  The folders you choose, the app's own log files (one per day, the last 7
  kept) and any card images you download also stay here. The app's log files
  can contain MTGO usernames, yours and your opponents'. Your sign-in and
  this device's API key are kept in the system keychain or, on Linux without
  one, in an obscured file in the app's data folder, which is not real
  encryption.

Sent when offline mode is off

  When your deck's archetype cannot be confidently detected on this
  computer, its decklist and format are sent to the API for an estimate. The
  app also checks for updates to the archetypes it holds by sending their
  IDs and versions, and asks for metagame shares and archetype decklists
  when the Metagame page, an archetype or the game overlay needs them.

  With the game overlay on (it is off by default), the app sends your
  current opponent's MTGO username and the format to look up their known
  archetype, and may send the cards they have revealed so far for an
  estimate. This feature sends a lookup as well as receiving one.

  After each match, the app sends your MTGO username and login ID, the match
  result, the format and a timestamp, the app version, your decklist and its
  detected archetype, your opponent's MTGO username and detected archetype,
  and any league or tournament token and round number. For every game it
  also sends the turn count, start and end times and your opening hand: the
  exact cards you kept, how many hands you mulliganed, whether you were on
  the play, and your opponent's mulligan count. Matches you enter by hand
  are not reported.

  Your opponent's decklist goes with that data: the cards the app saw them
  use, up to four copies of each. This is another player's data rather than
  yours, and it is submitted so the community archetype database stays
  current.

  Tournament observations are sent for any MTGO tournament event your client
  syncs, including events you only browse in the lobby rather than enter.
  Most of them carry a player roster for that event, containing other
  players' MTGO usernames, numeric login IDs and avatar IDs. Round by round
  they also carry everyone's standings (rank and points), elimination
  status, and the round's match pairings, including a match ID and token for
  every game in the round, exactly as MTGO broadcasts them rather than only
  the games you played. These are MTGO's own messages passed through
  unfiltered, so they can contain more than the list above, including a
  human readable event name that MTGO puts in the same raw message. As with
  the opponent decklist, this is other players' data rather than yours.

  A closing report is sent when an event ends, carrying one further MTGO
  login ID, which may identify another competitor or your own account.

  Per game, per card statistics are sent with your deck's archetype and the
  archetype they were observed against, the format, the result, whether you
  were on the play, whether it was a sideboarded game and the date. They
  carry no username or match ID. To show a tournament's real name, the app
  sends its MTGO event ID; the name is looked up from mymtgo's own
  tournament records, not read back from what you sent.

  None of this needs a MyMTGO account: signed out, it goes with this
  device's identifier (see below). Matches played while offline mode was on
  are sent once you turn it off.

Sent only while signed in

  Signing in opens mymtgo.com in your browser, which hands the result back
  to the app on this computer only. Cloud sync then runs every 30 minutes
  and shortly after each match, and downloads what your other devices have
  synced. It uploads your decks that are not archived (draft and sealed
  decks only with Supporter): their names, formats, every version's
  decklist, sideboard guides, matchup notes and archetype. For the decks you
  choose to sync, it also uploads their matches and leagues: results, games
  and timings, the cards each side played and saw, opening hands and
  mulligans, the game states replays are built from, your notes, your
  opponent's MTGO username and, for leagues, your drafts and picks.

  The app also tells MyMTGO which MTGO accounts (login ID and username) it
  has seen on this computer, so synced matches show under the right player.

  Sharing a replay, when you choose Share, uploads that match's game states,
  game log and sideboarding, its format, date and archetypes, and your MTGO
  login ID, with each opponent's name replaced by "Opponent". Your own
  username stays in it, and in the link. The replay opens on an unlisted
  page that anyone with the link can watch, without signing in; search
  engines are asked not to list it. You can switch the link off later, and
  it stops working within a few minutes.

  With "Publish live overlay" on, the app sends your league card for your
  current MTGO account: the event name and format, your record, the current
  match's game results, your deck's name, archetype and label, and a link to
  its art, plus your custom background image if you set one. It is sent on
  each change and every 30 seconds. Turning it off asks MyMTGO to remove it.

Sent always, even in offline mode

  MTGO card identifiers and card or token names, looked up against the API
  to get card names, mana costs and an image link so your decks keep
  rendering. No match, result or player data is attached to these lookups.

  Signed out, every request above, card lookups included, carries a
  generated device identifier and a short lived API key the app gets by
  registering that identifier. Registration keeps happening while offline
  mode is on because card lookups depend on it. Signed in, requests carry
  your account's sign-in token instead, so the API knows which account sent
  them.

Not affected by offline mode

  App updates. The app checks releases.mymtgo.com shortly after it starts
  and every hour, downloads a newer version in the background and installs
  it only when you choose. The check carries no account or device
  identifier; the server sees your IP address and whether you are on the
  stable or edge channel. Offline mode never blocks updates.

  Error reporting. Crash and error reports go to Sentry, a third party error
  tracking service, whatever offline mode is set to. There is no setting to
  turn them off. A report holds the error and where in the app it happened,
  the app version, your operating system and its version, your processor
  type and recent app log messages. Before a report is sent, the MTGO
  usernames the app knows or finds in it are replaced with "<player>" and
  your home folder with "~", and your computer's name and user details are
  left out. A report that cannot be cleaned this way is not sent. MyMTGO's
  error reporting service does not store your IP address.

  Card images. Once a card's image link is known, the app loads the image
  from Scryfall's servers rather than ours. Scryfall is a third party, and
  it sees your IP address and, in near real time, which cards you are
  looking at. This is outside our control. Turning on "Download card images
  locally" in Settings avoids it after the first download by keeping the
  images on your computer instead.

Only when you ask

  "Download report data" in Get help saves a zip file to a place you choose;
  nothing is uploaded. It holds the newest mtgo.log, the three newest MyMTGO
  log files and a summary (app version, update channel, operating system,
  watcher state, offline mode, whether you are signed in, and match and game
  counts). The logs are not cleaned, so they contain MTGO usernames, yours
  and your opponents'. It never holds the database, settings, deck files or
  sign-in details.

What offline mode gives up

  Archetype estimates and updates, metagame shares, opponent scouting,
  tournament names, cloud sync, replay sharing and the published overlay all
  rely on the traffic above, so expect them to go stale or become
  unavailable while offline mode is on. After you turn offline mode off, it
  cannot be turned on again until the next day.

Deleting your data

  Signing out removes your sign-in from this computer and stops cloud sync,
  replay sharing and the published overlay. It does not delete what MyMTGO
  already holds, and it does not stop match reports; offline mode does.
  "Delete all imported" on the Import page removes the matches imported from
  MTGO's history files. Turning off "Download card images locally" deletes
  the downloaded images.

  To remove everything from this computer, sign out, then uninstall. On
  Windows, tick "Delete the application data" in the uninstaller, or the
  data folder (%APPDATA%\com.mymtgo.desktop) stays. On Linux, delete
  ~/.local/share/com.mymtgo.desktop yourself. This device's API key stays in
  the system keychain after uninstalling; to remove it, delete the
  com.mymtgo.desktop entries in Credential Manager on Windows or in your
  keyring on Linux.
